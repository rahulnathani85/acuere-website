Registered Valuer Support for Merchant Bankers and Fund Managers

For the reports SEBI and the Companies Act require an independent Registered Valuer to sign: preferential issues, open offers, sweat equity, private placements and AIF portfolios.

An independent valuer you can bring in when the deal needs one

Merchant bankers, fund managers and company secretaries often reach a point in a transaction where the regulation needs a report from an independent Registered Valuer, usually against a board meeting or filing date that is already fixed. That is the work we do.

Reports that need an independent Registered Valuer

The situations we are most often brought in for.

Preferential issue above 5% or change in control

SEBI ICDR Regulation 166A. The floor price is the higher of the Regulation 164 price and the value in the Registered Valuer's report. Where control changes, the report also gives guidance on control premium.

Infrequently traded shares

SEBI ICDR Regulation 165. The price takes into account book value, comparable trading multiples and other customary parameters, and the issuer submits the Registered Valuer's certificate to the stock exchange.

Share swap in a preferential issue

SEBI ICDR Regulation 163(3). Non-cash consideration has to be a swap of shares, supported by an independent Registered Valuer's report.

Open offer for infrequently traded shares

Under the amended SEBI Takeover Regulations, the offer price for shares that are not frequently traded is determined by an independent Registered Valuer.

Sweat equity for know-how or IP

Under the SEBI SBEB Regulations, valuing intellectual property or know-how contributed for sweat equity needs an independent Registered Valuer.

Pre-IPO and private placements

A preferential allotment by an unlisted company under Section 62(1)(c) of the Companies Act needs a Registered Valuer's report on the issue price.

AIF portfolio valuation

Category I and II AIFs value their portfolios through an independent valuer meeting SEBI's eligibility criteria, at least every six months. Rahul meets those criteria. More on AIF valuation.

Schemes of arrangement

The listed entity files a Registered Valuer's report with the stock exchange, and the merchant banker's fairness opinion is based on it. More on listed company valuations.

How an engagement runs

Turned around to your board meeting or filing date. Tell us the date at the start and we plan the report back from it.

1. Independence check and scope

Before accepting, we confirm independence from the issuer, the allottees or acquirer and the fund, and agree which regulation the report has to satisfy.

2. A document list for that trigger only

We ask only for what the specific report needs, once, so the company is not chased for the same information twice.

3. Draft for factual review

You and the company review the draft for factual accuracy before signing. The value remains the valuer's independent conclusion.

4. Signed report with a VRN

The final report is signed by the Registered Valuer and carries a Valuation Report Number from the Registered Valuers Organisation, with workings available for the auditor and the exchange.
