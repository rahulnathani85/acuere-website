Who Can Be an Independent Valuer for an AIF?

SEBI's eligibility test, the 2024 rule for valuer firms, and the standards and deadlines that follow

By CA Rahul Nathani November 15, 2026 6 min read

Since SEBI's June 2023 valuation framework, the choice of an AIF's valuer is no longer just a commercial decision. The investment manager has to make sure the fund appoints an independent valuer who meets criteria SEBI has set, and the manager remains responsible for the valuation being true and fair. This note sets out who qualifies, when the valuer is needed, and what standard the valuation has to follow.

When an AIF needs an independent valuer

Under Regulation 23 of the SEBI (Alternative Investment Funds) Regulations, 2012:

  • Category I and II AIFs must have their investments valued by an independent valuer at least once every six months. This can be extended to once a year with the approval of at least 75% of the investors by value.
  • Category III AIFs must have their investments in unlisted securities and listed debt securities valued by an independent valuer for the purpose of calculating NAV.

SEBI's eligibility test

Regulation 23(4) requires the manager to ensure the AIF appoints an independent valuer who meets the criteria SEBI specifies. SEBI's circular of 21 June 2023, now part of Chapter 22 of the AIF Master Circular, sets three conditions. The valuer must:

  • not be an associate of the manager, sponsor or trustee of the AIF;
  • have at least three years of experience in valuing unlisted securities; and
  • either be a valuer registered with the IBBI who is also a member of ICAI, ICSI, ICMAI or the CFA Institute, or be a holding company or subsidiary of a SEBI-registered credit rating agency.

All three have to be met together. Registration with IBBI alone is not enough, and neither is experience alone.

Valuation firms: the September 2024 clarification

The 2023 wording created a practical problem for valuation firms. A firm can be registered with IBBI as a Registered Valuer Entity, but the individual professionals doing the work may not all hold ICAI, ICSI, ICMAI or CFA memberships. SEBI's circular of 19 September 2024 added clause 22.3.4 to the Master Circular to deal with this. Where the independent valuer is a partnership or company:

  • the firm must be a Registered Valuer Entity registered with IBBI; and
  • the person or persons it deputes to value the AIF's portfolio must be members of ICAI, ICSI or ICMAI, or hold the CFA Charter.

So for a firm, the professional membership requirement attaches to the people actually signing off the AIF work, not to every partner.

Which standard the valuation follows

The Master Circular splits the portfolio in two:

  • Securities for which valuation norms already exist under the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996 are valued on those norms.
  • Unlisted securities, and listed securities that are not traded or thinly traded, are valued under guidelines endorsed by an AIF industry association whose members make up at least 33% of SEBI-registered AIFs. The guidelines endorsed under this route are the IPEV Guidelines (International Private Equity and Venture Capital Valuation Guidelines).

The September 2024 circular also changed how a change of method is treated. A change in valuation methodology made to comply with this standardised approach, or a change within the prescribed guidelines, is no longer a "material change" that would require an exit option for dissenting investors. But where the method changes, the valuation on both the old and the new method has to be disclosed to investors.

Deadlines that run off the valuer's report

  • Performance benchmarking: valuations based on investee companies' audited data as of 31 March have to be reported to benchmarking agencies by 31 October each year. SEBI also expects the manager to build a timeline for investee companies to share audited accounts into the investment agreements.
  • NAV reporting to depositories: SEBI's circular of 6 February 2026 requires AIFs to report NAV for their units to the depositories. Where an independent valuer is appointed, the date of the valuer's report is treated as the valuation date for working out the reporting timeline. A late valuation report therefore means a late filing.

Questions to ask before appointing a valuer

  • Is the valuer, or the valuer firm, registered with IBBI, and in the Securities or Financial Assets class?
  • If it is a firm, which individuals will actually do your valuation, and do they hold ICAI, ICSI, ICMAI or CFA credentials?
  • Can the valuer show at least three years of valuing unlisted securities?
  • Is there any relationship with your manager, sponsor or trustee that could make the valuer an associate?
  • Does the valuer work to the IPEV Guidelines in practice, with calibration to the entry price and consistent methods from one period to the next, and document the reasons when a method changes?
  • Can the reports be delivered in time for your benchmarking and depository reporting dates?
