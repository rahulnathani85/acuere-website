The 2025 IPEV Guidelines: What Changes for Indian AIF Valuations

How SEBI's standardised approach routes AIF valuations through IPEV, and what to check before the first valuation date under the new edition

By CA Rahul Nathani November 24, 2026 6 min read

The IPEV Board published a new edition of the International Private Equity and Venture Capital Valuation Guidelines in December 2025. It applies to quarterly reporting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2026, with early adoption encouraged. For Indian AIFs the Guidelines are not just good practice: SEBI's standardised approach routes the valuation of most unlisted holdings through them. This note sets out how the SEBI rule works, what the 2025 edition changes, and what managers and valuers should check before the next valuation date.

Why IPEV matters for an Indian AIF

Chapter 22 of SEBI's AIF Master Circular, as amended by the circular of 19 September 2024, splits an AIF's portfolio in two:

  • Securities other than unlisted securities and non-traded or thinly traded listed securities are valued on the norms that apply to mutual funds under the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996.
  • Unlisted securities, and listed securities that are non-traded or thinly traded, are valued under guidelines endorsed by an AIF industry association whose members make up at least 33% of SEBI-registered AIFs. The association endorses the guidelines after taking into account the recommendations of SEBI's Alternative Investment Policy Advisory Committee. The guidelines endorsed under this route are the IPEV Guidelines.

For many Category I and II funds, that second bucket is most of the portfolio. So when IPEV changes, the standard that the fund's independent valuer works to changes with it.

What the 2025 edition changes

The core principles are unchanged. Fair value is still the price that would be received in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date, consistent with IFRS 13 and ASC 820. What the 2025 edition does is expand and sharpen the guidance in areas where practice had drifted. The changes most relevant to Indian portfolios are:

  • Calibration at each measurement date. Where the entry price was fair value, the valuation inputs should be calibrated to it, and that calibration should be rolled forward at each later measurement date, not done once and forgotten.
  • Price of recent investment is not a default. A recent round is a relevant input, but it cannot simply be carried forward as fair value without assessing what has changed since.
  • Hybrid instruments. A new dedicated section covers convertibles and other hybrid instruments, such as venture debt and convertible notes. For Indian funds, this is relevant to CCPS, CCDs and structured instruments, where the terms drive value as much as the business does.
  • Complex capital structures. There is more guidance on liquidation preferences and on allocating enterprise value across share classes, including where scenario-based, option pricing or hybrid methods may be more suitable than simpler approaches.
  • Secondary transactions. More guidance on discounts or premia observed in secondary trades, and on when such a trade is evidence of fair value.
  • Sustainability. The ESG section is renamed and expanded. Sustainability factors count where they are measurable and material, and are reflected through cash flows, discount rates or multiples rather than as a separate adjustment.
  • AI and automated models. These can support the process, but they do not replace professional judgement.

What the SEBI framework says about changing methods

The September 2024 circular deals with a practical worry about moving to a new standard. A change in valuation methodology made to comply with the standardised approach, or a change within the prescribed guidelines, is not treated as a "material change" that would require an exit option for dissenting investors. But where the method changes, the valuation on both the old and the new method has to be disclosed to investors.

So a fund that revisits its approach to, say, a CCPS holding in light of the 2025 hybrid instruments guidance does not trigger the material change process. It does have to show investors both numbers.

Points to settle before the next valuation date

  • Which edition applies. SEBI's rule refers to guidelines endorsed by the industry association, not to a named edition. Confirm the endorsed position with the association and record in the valuation policy which edition the fund follows and from which date.
  • Calibration trail. For each holding valued off an entry price, is there a documented calibration at entry and a roll-forward at each date since?
  • Holdings still at last round price. List investments carried at the price of the most recent round, and check what has changed in the company and the market since that round.
  • Instrument terms. For CCPS, CCDs and other hybrids, check that the conversion terms, liquidation preferences and anti-dilution rights are reflected in the value, not just the headline price per share.
  • Method changes. If any method is changing, plan the disclosure of old and new valuations to investors.
  • Timelines. The independent valuer's report date drives the fund's depository NAV reporting timeline, so build the extra documentation into the schedule.
