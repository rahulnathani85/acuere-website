POST 2 (19 Nov) - Control premium

When a listed company's preferential issue may change control, the registered valuer's report under SEBI ICDR Regulation 166A has to do more than give a price.

It also has to give guidance on control premium, worked out over and above the floor price.

The regulation doesn't say how. No method, no range, no minimum percentage. That's where most reports fall short. Control premium gets one line and a percentage taken from general literature, with nothing tied to the company or the deal.

What I think the board and the independent directors should expect:

- a clear statement of which figure sets the floor: the Reg 164 price, the valuer's price, or the Articles
- whether the allottee actually gets control, given the rights that pass
- what the new controller can realistically change in this business
- evidence from comparable Indian control transactions
- the open offer angle, if SAST is triggered
- a reasoned range, not a bare number

The report goes on the company's website and is referred to in the meeting notice, so shareholders read it too.

POST 3 (23 Nov) - AIF independent valuer

Since June 2023, an AIF's valuer can't just be anyone the manager is comfortable with. SEBI has set a test.

An individual independent valuer has to:
- be registered with IBBI as a Registered Valuer
- hold ICAI, ICSI or ICMAI membership, or the CFA charter
- have at least 3 years of experience valuing unlisted securities
- not be an associate of the manager, sponsor or trustee

Since September 2024, if the valuer is a firm, the firm itself has to be an IBBI Registered Valuer Entity, and the people doing the work need the same professional credentials.

Category I and II AIFs need this valuation at least every six months. Category III AIFs need it for unlisted securities and listed debt in their NAV. And with depository reporting now tied to the valuer's report date, the valuer's timeline affects the fund's reporting deadlines.
