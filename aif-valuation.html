Independent Valuation for AIF Portfolios

Half-yearly and NAV valuations of unlisted portfolio holdings under the SEBI AIF Regulations and the IPEV Guidelines, by an independent valuer who meets SEBI's eligibility criteria.

What SEBI requires of an AIF

Category I and II AIFs must have their investments valued by an independent valuer at least once every six months. This can be extended to once a year with the approval of at least 75% of investors by value. Category III AIFs need an independent valuer for their unlisted securities and listed debt securities when calculating NAV.

Securities not covered by the mutual fund valuation norms, which includes most unlisted equity, preference shares and convertibles, are valued under the IPEV Guidelines, the framework endorsed for AIFs under the SEBI master circular. The investment manager remains responsible for a true and fair valuation, so the valuer's work has to stand up to the fund auditor and to investors.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who can act as an independent valuer for an AIF?

Under the SEBI AIF master circular, an individual valuer must be registered with IBBI and be a member of ICAI, ICSI, ICMAI or the CFA Institute, must have at least three years of experience valuing unlisted securities, and must not be an associate of the manager, sponsor or trustee.

How often must an AIF portfolio be valued?

Category I and II AIFs: at least every six months, extendable to annually with approval of 75% of investors by value. Category III AIFs: for NAV calculation, with unlisted securities and listed debt securities valued by an independent valuer.

Which standard do you follow?

The IPEV Guidelines for unlisted and non-traded securities, as endorsed for AIFs under the SEBI master circular, and the mutual fund valuation norms where those apply.
