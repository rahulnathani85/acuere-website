Valuation Reports for Listed Companies

Independent Registered Valuer reports for preferential issues, schemes of arrangement and Ind AS reporting, written to be read by the board, the audit committee, the stock exchange and the auditor.

When a listed company needs a Registered Valuer

For most preferential issues, a listed company prices the shares on the SEBI ICDR formula and needs no valuation report. A Registered Valuer becomes mandatory at a few specific points: a large allotment or change in control, shares that are infrequently traded, an issue against non-cash consideration, and any scheme of arrangement filed with the stock exchange.

Where SEBI requires an independent valuation

The triggers below are the ones we are most often engaged for.

Preferential issue above 5% or change in control (Reg 166A)

An allotment that results in a change in control, or more than 5% of the post-issue fully diluted capital to one allottee or allottees acting in concert, needs a report from an independent Registered Valuer. The floor price is the higher of the Regulation 164 price and the value in the report. Where control changes, the report also gives guidance on control premium.

Infrequently traded shares (Reg 165)

Where shares are not frequently traded, the price is set by an independent Registered Valuer taking into account book value, comparable trading multiples and other parameters customary for valuing shares of such companies.

Issue for consideration other than cash (Reg 163(3))

Since January 2022, non-cash consideration in a preferential issue can only be a swap of shares. The swap has to be supported by a valuation report from an independent Registered Valuer, which is submitted to the stock exchanges where the issuer is listed.

Schemes of arrangement

Under the SEBI master circular on schemes, the listed entity files a valuation report from a Registered Valuer with the stock exchange, and the audit committee considers it. A SEBI-registered merchant banker then gives a fairness opinion on that valuation. We prepare the valuation and share exchange ratio, and work alongside the merchant banker.

Ind AS fair value

Fair value measurement under Ind AS 113, purchase price allocation under Ind AS 103, ESOP fair value under Ind AS 102, and financial instrument valuation under Ind AS 109, prepared so the statutory auditor can test it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does every preferential issue by a listed company need a Registered Valuer?

No. Frequently traded shares are usually priced on the Regulation 164 formula. A report becomes mandatory under Regulation 166A (change in control or more than 5% to an allottee), Regulation 165 (infrequently traded shares) and Regulation 163(3) (share swaps).

Who can issue the valuation report?

A valuer registered under Section 247 of the Companies Act, 2013, who is independent of the issuer and the allottees. Rahul Nathani is registered with IBBI for Securities or Financial Assets.

Do you also give the fairness opinion on a scheme?

No. Under SEBI's scheme framework the fairness opinion comes from a SEBI-registered merchant banker. We prepare the Registered Valuer report the opinion is based on.

Need an RV report for a preferential issue, a share swap or a scheme?

Share the board meeting date and the structure. We will confirm which ICDR rules apply and what the report must cover.
